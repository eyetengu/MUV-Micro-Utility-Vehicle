# MUV – Micro Utility Vehicle

### A Modular, Adaptable, Electric Utility Platform

## The Concept

The Micro Utility Vehicle (MUV) began as an exploration of electric mobility for robotics and personal transportation projects. 
What started with two electric hub motor wheels, a battery, and an idea has evolved into something considerably more interesting: 
     a lightweight, modular electric vehicle platform capable of serving many different purposes.

The underlying concept is simple:

**Build a small, adaptable vehicle around a modular chassis that can be configured to meet the needs of its operator.**

Rather than designing a vehicle for one specific purpose, the MUV is intended to provide a common structural and electrical 
platform that can evolve through interchangeable components, attachments, and configurations.

Depending on how it is equipped, the MUV could serve as:
- Personal neighborhood transportation.
- A property maintenance and yard-work vehicle.
- A homestead utility vehicle.
- A tool and equipment carrier.
- A recreational vehicle.
- A mobile robotics platform.
- A foundation for future autonomous utility applications.

The goal isn't to replace a full-sized tractor, automobile, or ATV. It is to provide an alternative for the countless smaller 
tasks where those machines are often unnecessarily large, expensive, or complicated.

## Design Philosophy

Several principles have emerged during the development of the MUV.

**1. Modularity**

The vehicle should be designed as a collection of functional assemblies rather than a permanently integrated collection of parts.

Major components should be removable, replaceable, and upgradeable without requiring extensive disassembly of the entire vehicle.

**2. Serviceability**

Maintenance should be considered during the design process, not after construction.

One example is the removable rear wheel assembly. 
The hub motor wheel, torque arms, and supporting tubing form a removable module that slides into larger square tubing incorporated into the chassis. 
This allows the rear wheel to be removed without dismantling the chassis.

**3. Adaptability**

The chassis should accommodate different configurations, accessories, and utility attachments.

The intention is to maintain a common underlying platform while allowing the end user to determine the vehicle's ultimate purpose.

**4. Simplicity**

Where practical, the design favors readily available materials, standard fasteners, bicycle components, and commonly obtainable electrical components.

The objective is to make the MUV approachable to individuals who enjoy building, modifying, and maintaining their own equipment.

**5. Iterative Development**

The MUV was not conceived as a finished engineering design.

It emerged through experimentation, fabrication, testing, and repeated refinement.

The architecture is intended to accommodate change. Early design decisions should not prevent future improvements.

## Chassis Architecture

The current prototype uses a modular chassis constructed primarily from perforated square steel tubing and angle iron.

The structural components are connected using 5/16-inch bolts of varying lengths rather than relying on permanent welded assemblies.

This approach provides several advantages:
- Components can be repositioned.
- Assemblies can be removed or replaced.
- Modifications require relatively basic tools.
- The structure can accommodate future revisions.
- Construction does not require specialized welding equipment.

The use of nested square tubing also provides opportunities for sliding and removable structural assemblies.

The rear wheel mounting system is an example of this approach.

## Major Vehicle Systems

The MUV is being developed around several distinct systems:

**Chassis**
- Main structural assembly.
- Square tubing and angle iron construction.
- Modular mounting points.
- Removable component interfaces.

**Rear Drive Module**
- Electric hub motor.
- Standard bicycle-style wheel and tire.
- Axle mounting and torque arms.
- Removable sliding mounting assembly.

**Front Steering Module**
- Bicycle front fork.
- Front wheel.
- Handlebars.
- Steering and braking components.

**Power System**
- Rechargeable battery.
- Motor controller.
- Main fuse and electrical distribution.
- Charging connection.

A future objective is to consolidate the battery, controller, fuse, and supporting electronics into a removable power enclosure.

**Operator Interface**
- Accelerator.
- Brake controls.
- Display/HUD.
- Lighting controls.
- Accessory switches.

**Body and Protection**
- Wood body panels.
- Front and side enclosures.
- Bottom protection.
- Rear enclosure.
- Splash shields.
- Seat and storage areas.

**Cargo and Utility**
- Storage compartments.
- Tool carrying capability.
- Potential trailer connections.
- Interchangeable utility attachments.

These systems are intended to remain independently serviceable wherever practical.

## Current Development Status

The initial prototype is operational and has already demonstrated considerable versatility.

It has been used for neighborhood transportation, property maintenance, yard work, navigating uneven terrain, and general recreational use.

One of the most encouraging discoveries has been how useful a small, maneuverable electric vehicle can be when performing tasks that involve 
frequent stopping, starting, repositioning, and moving around obstacles.

The prototype has also helped establish several important design principles that will influence subsequent versions.

Development is ongoing.

Areas of continued work include:
- Refinement of the chassis architecture.
- Improved modular component mounting.
- Development of a removable electronics enclosure.
- Electrical system organization.
- Control and wiring improvements.
- Braking and safety validation.
- Additional cargo and utility configurations.

## An Open-Source Project

The MUV is being developed with the intention of sharing the design, construction methods, discoveries, and lessons learned.

This repository is intended to become a collaborative resource for people interested in:
- DIY electric vehicles.
- Modular vehicle design.
- Small-scale utility transportation.
- Robotics and mobile platforms.
- Practical fabrication techniques.
- Open-source hardware.

The long-term vision is to make the MUV architecture accessible enough that others can build their own versions, modify the design, 
develop new attachments, and contribute improvements.

There is no expectation that everyone will build the same vehicle.

In fact, the opposite is encouraged.

A person building a neighborhood commuter may have very different requirements from someone building a property maintenance vehicle 
or a mobile robotics platform.

The underlying chassis architecture should provide a starting point for all of them.

## The Bigger Idea

The MUV is an exploration of what happens when we stop thinking about a vehicle as a single-purpose machine and start thinking about it as a configurable platform.

One chassis.

Multiple configurations.

Numerous possibilities.

The original prototype began with two motorized wheels and very little certainty about where the project would lead.

The next phase is about documenting what has been learned, refining the architecture, and making the concept reproducible.

**The goal isn't simply to build a vehicle. It's to create a platform that other people can take in directions I haven't even considered yet.**
